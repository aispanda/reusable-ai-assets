# Token-efficient stakeholder intake

Use this when someone has a website idea but is not a product, design, or technology specialist. Ask one short decision packet at a time. Recommend a default, explain its effect in business language, and stop asking once the next safe action is clear.

## Packet 1 — purpose

1. What should a visitor understand, trust, or do within one minute?
2. Who are the audiences? Rank them only if one must win a design conflict.
3. What proof makes the promise credible?
4. What is the smallest useful public launch?

Output: one-sentence promise, audience list, primary action, success measures, and a first-release hypothesis.

## Packet 2 — content and identity

1. New or existing name, identity, and brand?
2. Which content exists now: articles, services, work, assets, demos, résumé/team, policies, media?
3. Who writes, approves, corrects, and retires each content type?
4. Which voice, visual references, accessibility, language, cultural, or legal constraints matter?

Output: brand direction, content inventory, ownership, provenance, and approval rules. Never invent public claims or identity decisions.

## Packet 3 — capabilities

Ask only about capabilities that affect architecture or cost:

- public search/filtering or large collections;
- forms, newsletter, booking, analytics, or external embeds;
- accounts, private data, uploads, payments, comments, contributions, chat, or moderation;
- real-time updates, personalisation, API, database, CMS, localisation, or offline use.

Output: `Launch`, `Grow`, `Community`, or `Not planned` for each requested capability, plus an activation trigger. A future idea is not a launch requirement.

## Packet 4 — delivery constraints

Ask for launch window, recurring-cost ceiling, domain/hosting preference, maintainer skill, security/privacy duties, and who may approve source-control accounts, repositories, spend, deployment, DNS, or third-party services.

Output: reversible assumptions, hard constraints, and explicit approval owners. If source control is not ready, record provider, account/organization owner, backup owner, MFA/recovery, repository visibility, licence, secrets boundary, and deployment access before code begins.

## Response format

For every unresolved decision, use at most:

1. **Decision** — what must be chosen.
2. **Scenario** — what the visitor experiences.
3. **Options** — no more than three credible choices with concise pros, cons, cost, scalability, and reversibility.
4. **Recommendation** — one choice and why.
5. **Answer needed** — accept, amend, reject, or supply one missing fact.

Do not request visual preferences before purpose, content, and trust are understood. Use examples or a small visual when they reduce explanation. Record answers once in the project profile; do not ask again unless the facts change.
