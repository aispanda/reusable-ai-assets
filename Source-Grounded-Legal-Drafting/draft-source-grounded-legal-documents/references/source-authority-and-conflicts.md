# Source authority and conflicts

Use this reference when a legal drafting task has multiple sources, a precedent, or a possible legal error.

## Classify sources

| Role | Controls | Does not control |
|---|---|---|
| User instruction | Scope, deliverable, permitted changes | Missing facts or incorrect law |
| Factual source | Matter-specific facts expressly stated | Unstated facts, motives, or legal conclusions |
| Current primary law | Mandatory rules, current citations, formalities | Commercial choices left to the parties |
| Template or prior draft | Structure, defined terms, negotiated provisions | Facts from another matter or superseded law |
| Helpful summary | Search leads and issue spotting | A legal proposition until verified |

## Resolve conflicts

1. Identify the exact conflicting propositions and their sources.
2. Verify current law from an official source if the conflict is legal.
3. Preserve the user's commercial objective where lawful.
4. Correct an outdated title, year, repeal, commencement, savings rule, section, or citation.
5. Do not silently alter economics, parties, property, scope, remedies, or risk allocation.
6. If the conflict needs a commercial choice, mark the clause for decision instead of choosing for the parties.

## Missing information

Use a precise execution blank or `TBD - source required` for a missing party detail, date, amount, property identifier, schedule, notice address, valuation, consent, release, or approval. State which source would resolve it.

## Research stop rule

Research the smallest set of questions that can change the draft. Stop when primary sources establish the current rule and no unresolved status issue affects the document. Do not add secondary commentary merely to lengthen a source list.
